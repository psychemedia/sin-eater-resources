# Introduction

I first came across the tradition of "the sin-eater" in a story told by Welsh traditional storyteller Ceri Phillips. Digging into the history of it, the term seems to originate with a brief descrption by 17th century antiquarian John Aubrey, who provides a brief description of a sin-eater who live in "a cottage on Rosse highway".

Whilst I have as yet found no other recorded folklore describing the sin-eater of Ross, there was a tale that weaves a sin-eater into another local tale, of how Old Maid's Walk, a lane at the top-side of town that connects Walford Road to the Church, got its name.

From fragments of two other tales provided by "Uncle Tim", I also tracked down a couple of other historical tales from the forest, that I need to start working on, and forgetting, in order to get a tale out of them. For what is a story if not the thing that is left when the facts have been forgotten...
